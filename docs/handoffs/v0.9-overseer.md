## How the waves have run (what worked)
Carried from `docs/handoffs/v0.8-overseer.md`: at most two subagents at once, each in its own
`make task` folder; the overseer designs, briefs, reviews the diff and runs `make land` itself, one
land at a time; seams land first; briefs carry the decided design; the full gate
(`LAND_GATE=check make land`) for a task whose tests sit outside `check-fast`.

Workers on this host (`workerctl`, off the overseer's own usage; as of 2026-11-07):
- Check `workerctl usage` first; it names the model behind each tier and what quota or balance is left.
- Prefer the `agy` provider's tiers while their quota lasts: its fast tier for mechanical work, its
  strong tier for implementation from a written design, codebase exploration and "today"-claim checks
  against the code. Its top tier falls back to the strong tier when its quota is empty.
- Spend the prepaid `deepseek` balance (`deepseek:flash` mechanical, `deepseek:pro` implementation)
  after that; each run's cost shows in `workerctl status`.
- Same rules as subagents: each worker in its own `make task` folder, named under "In flight"; its brief
  carries what it needs (workers read `AGENTS.md` only); block on `workerctl wait`, never poll; review
  its diff as a subagent's.
- Host etiquette (S-291, AGENTS.md): never put artificial load on the host (no CPU burners or stress loops; slow the
  browser with Playwright CPU throttling to imitate a slow device), and stop every background process you start before
  you end a turn or report (`pgrep -P` / `ps`). Put both lines in every worker brief.
