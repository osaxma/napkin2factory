## Section verdicts
- Part A, using it: **CONCERN**. The full path worked (idea, board, promotion, release, change, wiring,
  choice, release, diff, publish) with no command typed by the owner. One BLOCKER found for any change
  request that removes something (B-1).
- Part B, the foundation: **CONCERN** (B10, B11 and B9 are measured misses; B2/B13/B14 not run).

## Blockers
1. **A change request that removes a part, parameter or joint bricks the product.** After
   `n2f ecr apply`, every command (validate, lint, build, check) errors because `changes/ECR-001.yaml`'s
   own stored impact lists the removed URIs (`part://wall-anchor`, `iface://wall-mount`, `param://anchor-*`)
   and the loader resolves them. `src/napkin2factory/ecr/lifecycle.py:302` (`fresh = load_product`) runs
   straight after the patch applies, so apply aborts half-done: status stays `approved`, model changed.
   Fix: do not resolve URIs in an ECR's `impact` (or record removed ones as plain strings), and make apply
   transactional. A17's own premise ("wall brackets gone") needs this to work. My workaround: strip those
   URIs from the ECR before apply.

## Concerns (one line each, with a concrete fix)
- C1. A requirement `verify: {kind: interface}` on a `cable-entry` with `passes: []` FAILS ("no circuit passes through this entry yet"), yet `roles/design.md:206` says to leave entries with `passes: []`; `docs/electrical.md:191` calls an empty `passes` "worth a look". Make the interface verify kind warn, or document the `manual` workaround.
- C2. `n2f discard` (answering y) silently deleted the filed and approved ECR and its patch: `ecr new`/`approve` leave no snapshot. Snapshot on each ECR step, or have `discard` name the files it removes first (`cli/snapshot.py:43`).
- C3. `n2f ecr apply` also leaves a sign-off citing a removed part (REQ-011 cited `part://wall-anchor`) as a validation error; the impact lists "must be redone" but does not say the file must be deleted. Say so in the impact report.
- C4. The impact over-predicts sign-offs: it listed 6 invalid after the wiring change, 3 actually needed re-signing (REQ-004, 009, 011, 015 stayed valid); and for the bench change it said 5, then 7 were needed. Compute the list from the same evidence keys the check uses.
- C5. `src/napkin2factory/templates/_common/roles/pm.md:74` and `docs/napkin.md:61` say each massing call's "bottom centre" sits at the origin; `bracket_l` starts at x = 0 (corner), widening my first board from 800 to 1180 mm. Say so in `docs/kit.md` massing table or centre it.
- C6. `docs/kit.md:24` lists `vent{...,size,...}` without saying `size` is the slot width (a list `[4 mm, 60 mm]` is refused) and the `frame` profile literal is `square-tube`, not `square` (found only by build error, not `n2f validate`). Add the argument types to `docs/kit.md`.
- C7. Kit args refuse `{expr: ...}` ("expected a length, got {'expr'...}") so a derived size needs a helper parameter (door-height); `docs/product-model.md` says expressions are allowed in frames only. State it in `roles/design.md`.
- C8. The owner's region (UK) has circuits on file but no conductor table: `n2f validate` warns "region 'UK' has no conductor table"; I borrowed EU rows. Add UK rows or say in `docs/electrical.md` which regions can be checked.
- C9. The wiring checks charge the whole circuit's current to every conductor in it, so a fan branch off the strip failed at 13.1 A (0.75 mm2). Either check branches by their own load or say in `docs/electrical.md` to split circuits.
- C10. `promote` sets the other cards `status: rejected` and DEC-001 says "Rejected alternatives" (`concepts/promote/verb.py:180`), against the rule that the owner never rejected them; also `--by` needs a name the agent may not know (I used "the owner").
- C11. Release package lacks an assembly STEP and any GLB (only `build/`), though the brief and the guide say 3D models are in the package; list what the package holds in `docs/ecr-loop.md`.
- C12. Interview-time friction: the product's roles ask for 3-5 directions before most facts; I invented a budget in the card notes from the script (T), the guide never says to ask budget before the board. Also the sign-off rows ask the owner to attest edges/anchors of a cabinet not yet built; the agent had to reword them as "checks to do later". Provide "to do at delivery" rows that do not block release.
- C13. First `n2f doctor`/`build` is 3-4 minutes of silence on macOS (kernel scan); guide warns of "one to two minutes". Raise the guide's number.
- C14. B9/B10/B11 are measured misses (8:29 vs 3 min here, 9 files over 600 lines, 30 of 87 commits without a journal id); fix the numbers in `STATUS.md` or the commits' discipline.
- C15. Playwright is "optional" and absent in the tool environment: `concept build` printed "styled screenshots unavailable", so no styled picture on the first board until `n2f build` later produced them; the guide's image step silently degrades.

## Notes on what worked
Disclaimer, ask-before-install, plain-word interview, red cards with reasons, DEC for every choice,
the impact-first change loop, the choice raised only after a build, the regenerated installation manual,
the diff and the publish folder all behaved as the README promises. The checks were honest: they
failed real arithmetic (13.1 A on a 13 A socket) and left every safety row to a person.

## Closing paragraph
Would I hand this to a tinkerer today? Yes, with one warning: it carried a non-engineer from an idea to
two released Revisions, a diff and a share folder without the owner touching a file, and its checks
refused real mistakes; but any change that removes a part (blocker 1) leaves the product unusable until
someone edits the change request by hand, and a tinkerer would not know how. Would I add a domain
tomorrow? Not yet: I did not run B2 (stopped on the usage limit), so I cannot say a 1091-line
`docs/domains.md` is enough; the electrical domain, which was added that way, did work for me end to
end once my conductor and region gaps (C8, C9) were worked around, but the evidence for the contract
is indirect. I would first fix blocker 1 and C1, then try the toy-domain exercise.
