# Review gate I (v0.7): engineering and honesty

Reviewed: clean clone of `/Users/wakeel/napkin2factory`, HEAD `3139a00` ("M4: the test-stand benchmark product, Tier 2 (W9, S-222)"), at `<scratch>/repo`. Builds and experiments on copies under `.../gate-i/work/`. Book and standard citations could not be checked offline; where I say a citation looks wrong it is from memory and marked so. Everything numeric below I ran or recomputed by hand.

## Verdict: NOT READY

Two blockers. (1) `joints.frame_racking(base_fixity="fixed")` uses a wrong formula that under-reports sway, by up to a factor 3 (checked against a stiffness-matrix solve); the shipped test only asserts "less than pinned". (2) The product `region` in `n2f.yaml` never reaches the cost screen: a product declared `region: EU` still gets US bands, in USD, with no region or date on the owner-facing cost line, so "dated regional costs" is half-built and the silent default is the one the brief asked about. Both fixes are small. Around them the calc pack is mostly sound: the formulas I hand-checked (11 of them) are right, wrong units raise, the three benchmarks build green and pass their oracles, the oracle judges outcomes not ids, and `lite` ceremony relaxes paperwork only. The weak spots are honesty of provenance (cases labelled "external/published" that are the author's own arithmetic; load-case sources that are plausible-sounding rather than specific), the table benchmark's central load-path assumption, and a tag-based ceremony that a wrong tag defeats.

**BLOCKER B1. `frame_racking` fixed-base branch is wrong.** `calckit/joints/frames.py` (fixed branch, near the end of `frame_racking`):
`delta_rigid = (P h^3 / (12 E I_c)) * ((k_ratio + 2) / (6 k_ratio + 1))`. Slope-deflection gives `(3 k + 2)/(6 k + 1)` (k = (I_b/L)/(I_c/h)). Checks: a rigid beam (k to infinity) must give two fixed-fixed columns, P h^3/(24 E I_c); the code gives a third of that. I confirmed with a 2D frame stiffness-matrix solve (P 100 N, h = L = 1 m, E 200 GPa, I_c 1e-6 m^4):

| k | code | FE solve | derived (3k+2)/(6k+1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.01786 mm | 0.02976 mm | 0.02976 mm |
| 1e6 | 0.00694 mm | 0.02084 mm | 0.02083 mm |

The same FE solve reproduces the pinned branch exactly (0.125 mm at k = 1). So the error is unconservative (sway too small, 40 % at k = 1). The only test is `tests/unit/test_joint_calcs.py:248`, `sway_fixed < sway_rigid`, which a wrong formula passes. No shipped example uses `fixed`, so no number in the benchmarks is affected today. Fix: replace `(k_ratio + 2.0)` with `(3.0 * k_ratio + 2.0)`; add a reference case at k = 1 (0.02976 mm in the numbers above) and one at the rigid-beam limit; check the semi-rigid term `P h^2/(4 K_theta)` the same way.

## Q2. Structural screen and benchmark specs

Screen (`concepts/screens/structural.py`) versus its own row text, run on the table and test-stand cards (`build/concepts/*/screening.json`):

- Table C-002 sag row says "the top sags 1.54 mm (computed) ... with the two long top rails sharing it (E·I of top + rails), self weight 282 N": I reproduce 1.378 + 0.165 = 1.544 by hand. The spec's 1.58 includes the rails' own weight (6.4 kg); the screen counts only the top's weight (282 N, "self weight" in the row). Difference 2.5 %, inside the oracle tolerance, but the row's "self weight" is the top's, not the table's. OK, minor.
- Yield row "factor 34.7": spec 34.0, same cause. OK.
- Buckling row: "one leg carrying its share of total weight plus applied loads (2072 N)": the code adds the full centre load and the full edge load to one leg (`vertical = leg_weight + applied`), so "its share" is wrong wording: it is the worst case, which is conservative (factor 29.6 vs spec 38.3 at 1583 N).
- Tip-over 3.88 vs 3.92: matches the model at the card's massing.
- Test-stand C-001 deflection 1.82 mm vs spec 1.90 mm: the screen takes one arm; the spec adds the base arm and the column stretch (the oracle row says so; the owner-facing row says "the arm's tip deflects"). Under-reports 4 %. And its yield row says "22.9 MPa of bending stress ... factor 6.32", spec's 5.76 adds axial; the row text is accurate (bending only).
- The deflection factor: the screen applies the cyclic row (1.25) whenever `cycles` is on the card (test-stand C-001: "limit ÷ sag is 0.11, 1.25 needed, cyclic case"); the test-stand SPEC §1 says the load is quasi-static and uses the static row. Different judgement, no effect on any outcome.

**CONCERN C7. The generic screen mis-describes cards it cannot model.** Table C-001 and C-003 (no frame kit) show `structural-deflection skip: not run: the column and the arm are different materials`. They are neither a column nor an arm. The rejection of C-001 actually comes from the *example's own* screen (`table-sag`, in `examples/table/model/screens/`, "the top sags 14.3 mm (computed)"), which is the right place under F10. Fix: when the pair matcher finds a column and an arm only by shape heuristics, say "this does not look like a column with an arm" instead of a materials message, or skip silently. Also the test-stand promoted portal (C-002): `structural-deflection skip: not run: ...` and the card is "promoted, passes every screening check" because the structural check was not run (B-0210 admits no portal case). The skip row is visible, so this is not hidden, but "promoted" for the stiff-by-design portal rests on the finished product's calc only.

**CONCERN C8. The table benchmark's load path is simplified exactly where the margin is thin.** SPEC §3 models top + two long rails as one beam along the length with EI added ("the top's local dishing between the long rails is not included"). The top's grain runs along the length, so across it the stiffness is a fraction of E_L (from memory of Wood Handbook elastic ratios, roughly 0.07-0.18, i.e. about 0.9-2.2 GPa). A strip across the 650 mm between rail lines (width 650, thickness 30) under 1471.5 N: delta = F L^3/(48 E I) = 2.6 to 6.4 mm for E_T 2.2 to 0.9 GPa (1.5 GPa gives 3.8 mm); cross-grain bending stress about 2.45 MPa (M = 239 kN·mm, Z = 97 500 mm^3) against a perpendicular-to-grain strength of the order of a few MPa. The promoted card may not meet its own 2.5 mm requirement under a point load. Fix: state the assumed contact patch of the 150 kg (a patch spreads the load and shortens the dishing), add a cross-grain bending line to §4.1, or require the top to be fixed to the rails with a stiffener; at minimum say in §1 that the sag assumes the load reaches the rails.

- A mains motor with no electrical model (no `supply.yaml`) cannot be seen by the wiring (`hazards()` returns `[]` when there is no supply, and also when the voltage cannot be read: `except Exception: return None` in `_volts`).
- With the electrical model, the test-stand and kiln derive `standard` with plain reasons ("it runs on mains: the supply is 120 V AC (given), above extra-low voltage; ... mains voltage is named by ..."). OK.
- Sign-offs: I traced every use of the level (`lint.py`, `bom/validation.py`, `rulepacks/checks.py`, `drawings/engine.py`, `report/builder_common.py`, `domains/mechanical/__init__.py`). It touches tolerance-class defaults, the manual-page rule, and empty report sections. No sign-off, provenance or safety check reads it. The docstring claim holds. OK. The table copy still shows "1 example sign-off (not a review)" in `n2f check`, labelled.

Is `tip-over` non-raising defensible for furniture? Mostly yes: raising only changes paperwork, and the requirement keeps its safety flag and sign-off. But the reason in `hazards.yaml` ("an everyday product's stability check keeps its sign-off without raising the ceremony") sits next to the table and stand rows; tip-over is the main severe-injury hazard for domestic furniture (children), so the one-page `build-and-care` manual is the only place the anchoring advice must live. Not a blocker; see C10.

**CONCERN C10. Suggest: lint warn when a safety requirement's statement mentions a quantity in V above the extra-low limit, or words `mains`, `230 V`, `live`, while no raising kind is tagged; and make `_volts` returning `None` raise a ceremony reason instead of lite ("the supply voltage could not be read").

## Q4. Region (S-200) and costs (S-199)

Fails loudly: `cost_band("woodworking", region="JP")` raises `table 'cost_bands' has no rows for region 'JP'; regions on file: EU, US`. A manifest `region: JP` is refused at load (validated against the wiring regions). OK.

**BLOCKER B2. The product's region never reaches the cost screen; a silent US default remains.** `concepts/screens/generic.py:235`: `card_region = getattr(card, "region", None)`. `ConceptCard` (`core/model/concepts.py`) has no `region` field, so this is always `None`, and `calckit/mass_cost.py:cost_band` with `region=None` "returns the first band on file for the process class without region filtering" (docstring). Experiment: copy of the table with `region: EU` in `n2f.yaml`, `n2f concept build --all` printed `C-002 holds up: ... 606–981 USD (estimate)` and the cost row `the cost, 570 USD to 945 USD, is within the 1600 USD budget`: US numbers in USD, byte-identical to the US run. `manifest.region` is read only by the electrical domain (`domains/electrical/model/refs.py`). Also, the owner-facing cost lines carry neither region nor date: estimates say "rough cost of the rough shapes from the cost bands; to be checked" (no "US, 2026-10/2026-11"), the report says "cost low 606 USD (estimate)". The code to print `"{region} cost bands per process ({dates})"` exists in `generic.py:273-285`, but only when `card_region` is not None, i.e. never. Latent crash behind it: with a region wired in, an EU band is in EUR and the screen does `c.low.to("USD")`: reproduced `DimensionalityError: Cannot convert from 'EUR' ([currency_eur]) to 'USD' ([currency])`. Also `CostBand` defaults `region="US"` for an unknown band (`cost_band("welding", region="EU")` returns `known=False` with `region='US'`). EU has 2 process classes of the 12 US ones, so an EU product would see "no price range on file" for nearly everything.
Fix: pass `manifest.region` into the screens (`ScreenValues` or the card context), print "{region} bands, {as_of}" on the cost line and the report cost line whenever bands are read, handle the currency (sum per currency or refuse), make `region=None` print "no region declared: US bands used" instead of silence, and give the EU table the other process classes or have the screen say which are missing for that region.

Dated: every `cost_bands.yaml` row has `as_of` ("2026-10" or "2026-11") and `verify: true`; staleness is flagged after 24 months, shown only when a region is given (so never today). The test-stand and table specs do quote "US, as of 2026-11" in their own text.

**CONCERN C11. Some bands are the examples' own estimates, not market data.** The US rows for `sheet-metal`, `cut-sheet`, `cut-block`, `fiber-board`, `welding`, `machining`, `cots-controls` (`as_of: "2026-10"`) take their ranges from "a product's per-part estimates (basis estimate)" ("a door tray 60 USD / 7.50 kg, a controller lid 15 USD / 0.245 kg ... a stand, welded ..., 140 USD / 7.17 kg"): the kiln's guesses become the package's bands, and two are single-point "bands" (`low = high`: cut-sheet 61.3, welding 19.5). The test-stand then prices a 2 kN actuator, a load cell and a PSU with the `cots-controls` band drawn from kiln controls (35-120 USD each; SPEC §4.7 admits it). The kiln names in package data are also an F9/F10 smell (Q7). The two-order spread (sheet-metal 8.0-67.6 USD/kg) is honest as a band but makes "cost <= 1600 USD at the high end" mean little. New 2026-11 rows (woodworking 9-22 USD/kg, cnc-routing 35-120 USD/m^2, laser 40-150, extrusion 15-45 USD/m, 3d-print 25-80 USD/kg) are all "typical shop rates" with no named basis; magnitudes are plausible for the US except that 25-80 USD/kg for FDM looks low for a service bureau (not verified). Fix: label the first group "estimates from one product" in the owner-visible text, not "cost bands".

**Q4 verdict: BLOCKER B2; CONCERN C11.**
