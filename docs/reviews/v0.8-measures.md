## 2. Warm incremental rebuild after a one-parameter change (section 2.1)

v0.7's method: a fresh copy, `n2f build` once (cold), once more with nothing changed, then three runs, each after raising the parameter by 1 mm over the last, `n2f build` end to end; median of three.

| Tier | Example | Parameter | Cold build | Nothing changed | Three runs | Median | v0.7 median | v1.0 target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `examples/table` | `table-height` 750 mm | 19.3 s | 2.8 s | 20.5 / 19.2 / 19.4 s | 19.4 s | 14.4 s | under 1 s |
| 2 | `examples/test-stand` | `column-length` 760 mm | 44.2 s | 3.1 s | 22.4 / 21.4 / 22.3 s | 22.3 s | 19.4 s | set from the measurement |
| 3 | `examples/kiln` | `fiber-thickness` 125 mm | 81.8 s | 5.0 s | 64.0 / 59.9 / 61.6 s | 61.6 s | 50.8 s | set from the measurement |

Reading it: nothing changed is now 2.8 / 3.1 / 5.0 s against 8.2 / 8.7 / 11.2 s at v0.7 (R12's work shows), but a one-parameter change is 35 %, 15 % and 21 % slower than at v0.7 and about 19 times over tier 1's target. These series ran at load 3.3 to 4.2, and cold builds differ between this section and section 1 by up to 30 % (tier 1 14.9 s against 19.3 s), so the slowdown is real in direction only if it exceeds that noise; the tier 1 figure (+5 s) does, the other two are within it. The parameters are the same as v0.7's, which reach nearly every part, so the warm run stays close to the cold one; a parameter reaching one part was not measured. Tier 3 took under 10 minutes in all, so it was run.
